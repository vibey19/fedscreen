# Assumptions and simulation parameters

## The data is simulated

Every individual-level record in this project is generated by
`R/simulate.R`. There is no real screening data here, and no real person is
described by any row.

**All parameters below are invented.** They were chosen so the simulated
programme has a realistic shape: most invited people take part but not all, a
few percent of tests come back positive, not everyone with a positive test
goes on to colonoscopy, and findings get rarer the more serious they are. They
are not real Finnish figures, they are not drawn from any published table, and
no published source is claimed for any specific number here. They must not be
read as real statistics, or used to describe any real programme.

The three sites are made different on purpose so the dashboard has a genuine
comparison to show. Site B is written as a weak programme, site C as a strong
one, and site A sits between them. That contrast is a design choice, not a
finding.

## Shape of the simulated cohort

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Sites | Site A, Site B, Site C |
| Screening years | 2021, 2022, 2023 |
| Sex | female, male, split 50/50 in every stratum |
| Age groups | 55-59, 60-64, 65-69, 70-74, 75-79 |
| Rows | One per invited person per screening year |
| Total rows | 94,500 (36,000 + 27,000 + 31,500) |

A person invited in two different years appears as two rows with two different
ids. The simulation does not follow the same person across rounds. This is a
simplification, and it is the reason the interval cancer indicator here is
simpler than a registry's would be (see "Known simplifications" below).

Columns that do not apply to a person are left empty (`NA`, which becomes SQL
`NULL` in the database) rather than being set to `FALSE` or zero. For example,
`colonoscopy_done` is empty for anyone who never had a positive FIT, and
`finding` is empty for anyone who never had a colonoscopy. This is so a later
count cannot quietly treat "does not apply" as "no".

### Age group shares

Share of each site-year's invited people in each age group:

| Age group | Share |
| --- | --- |
| 55-59 | 2% |
| 60-64 | 33% |
| 65-69 | 32% |
| 70-74 | 30% |
| 75-79 | 3% |

The main invited range is 60 to 74. The two edge groups are deliberately tiny,
standing in for a small pilot extension at each end. They are small so that
some result cells contain only a handful of people, which is what makes the
small-number suppression rule visibly do something.

## Per-site parameters

| Parameter | Site A | Site B | Site C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Random seed | 101 | 102 | 103 |
| Invited per year | 12,000 | 9,000 | 10,500 |
| Participation (base) | 0.66 | 0.52 | 0.74 |
| FIT positivity (base) | 0.055 | 0.072 | 0.045 |
| Colonoscopy completion | 0.93 | 0.80 | 0.96 |
| Cancer, per colonoscopy | 0.045 | 0.038 | 0.050 |
| Advanced adenoma, per colonoscopy | 0.22 | 0.19 | 0.24 |
| Adenoma, per colonoscopy | 0.25 | 0.24 | 0.26 |
| Interval cancer, per negative FIT | 0.0006 | 0.0009 | 0.0005 |

Each site has its own seed so that regenerating one site does not change the
random draws of the other two.

"Base" means the value before the multipliers below are applied. The remaining
share of colonoscopies (for site A, 1 minus 0.045 minus 0.22 minus 0.25, so
about 49%) finds nothing.

## Multipliers

Each person's probability is the site base value multiplied by the values
below for their sex, age group and year. A multiplier of 1 means no change.
They set the direction of the usual gradients: women take part slightly more
often, men test positive and have findings more often, and both positivity and
findings rise with age. Participation drops at the two edge age groups because
they sit outside the main invited range. The small rise in participation across
the three years stands in for a programme settling in.

## What the simulated data comes out at

These are results, not inputs. They are what the parameters above produce, and
they are listed here so the numbers in the dashboard can be checked against
what was asked for.

| Indicator | Site A | Site B | Site C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Participation | 64% to 68% | 51% to 54% | 72% to 76% |
| FIT positivity | 5.4% to 6.0% | 7.2% to 7.8% | 4.5% to 4.6% |
| Colonoscopy completion | 93% to 94% | 76% to 78% | 95% to 96% |
| Cancers found, per year | 13 to 22 | 10 to 18 | 11 to 21 |
| Interval cancers, per year | 3 to 9 | 1 to 5 | 3 to 8 |

Interval cancers are rare by design, so once the data is broken down by year,
sex and age group almost every interval cancer cell is small enough to be
suppressed. That is realistic. Registries report rare events over longer
periods and wider groups for exactly this reason.

## The small-number suppression rule

Before a site exports anything, every count from 1 to 4 is removed.

- A count of 1, 2, 3 or 4 is replaced by an empty value and the row is flagged
  with `suppressed = TRUE`.
- Anything calculated from a suppressed count goes with it: the estimate and
  both confidence limits are emptied too. Publishing the proportion and the
  denominator would hand back the numerator (0.25 of 8 is 2).
- A count of 0 is published. Zero says nobody is in the cell, so there is
  nobody to identify.
- A count of 5 or more is published.
- The flag matters as much as the removal. Without it, a dashboard cannot tell
  "too small to show" apart from "no data", and the two mean different things.
  Suppressed cells are shown as "suppressed", never as zero.

The rule is in `R/suppress.R`. The threshold of 5 is the one the brief asks
for. Registries differ on the exact number, and the right one for real data
would be whatever the data holder's disclosure policy says.

There is a final check in `R/local_pipeline.R`, `assert_no_small_counts()`,
which refuses to write the export at all if any count between 1 and 4 is still
present. It should be impossible to reach. It is there because the cost of
being wrong is a privacy breach, not a wrong number, so the pipeline should
stop rather than publish.

### What this rule does not do

- **No secondary suppression.** If a table shows a total and all but one of its
  parts, the missing part can be worked out by subtraction. A registry would
  suppress a second cell to block that. This project does not, and the six
  breakdown levels it exports overlap, so some suppressed values could be
  recovered by combining them. That is a known gap, kept out on purpose to stay
  small, and it would have to be closed before anything like this touched real
  data.
- **It does not protect the derived numerator of PPV for advanced neoplasia.**
  That numerator is cancers plus advanced adenomas, so it can be published when
  the sum is 5 or more even if one part is smaller than 5. The parts are
  suppressed in their own rows, and only the sum is released, so nothing small
  is published directly.
- **No rounding or perturbation.** Published counts are exact. Some registries
  also round counts to the nearest 5, or add small random noise.

## Known simplifications

- **Each row is one invitation, not one followed person.** Nobody is linked
  across screening years, so this project cannot say anything about repeat
  participation or about what happens to a person over several rounds.
- **Interval cancer is a flag, not a follow-up.** A real interval cancer is a
  cancer diagnosed in the window between a negative screen and the next
  scheduled round, which needs linkage to a cancer registry and a date. Here it
  is simply drawn as a rare event among people with a negative FIT. The rate it
  produces is of a believable size, but the mechanism behind it is much simpler
  than the real one.
- **No age standardisation.** Rates are crude. Because the age mix is fixed to
  be the same at all three sites, site comparisons are not distorted by age
  here, but that is a property of the simulation and not something that would
  hold for real sites.
- **No screening history.** First-round and later-round participants are not
  distinguished, although in a real programme their positivity and detection
  rates differ.
- **One test, one pathway.** FIT only, colonoscopy only, colorectal cancer
  only.
